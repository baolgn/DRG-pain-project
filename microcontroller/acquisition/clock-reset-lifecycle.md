# Acquisition clocks, crossings and reset lifecycle

**ACQ-R1-CDC1, supplement 1 to ACQ-R1 revision 4.** These are normative implementation requirements, not implemented CDC or reset RTL. The [contract](contract.md), [timing budget](timing-budget.md) and [frozen formats](formats.md) remain applicable. ACF1 still encodes contract revision 4; the external qualification manifest must additionally identify this supplement and the implemented clock/reset/crossing design. No record, block or descriptor byte changes here.

The required invariant is that **only an owned, qualified operation can create a trigger, record, acknowledgment or ownership transition**. A reset edge, synchronizer initialization, empty flag, timer expiry, restored clock or old RAM contents cannot create one. The logical rules below preserve that invariant under arbitrary clock ordering and interruption. Their physical applicability still requires timing, reset-domain and metastability qualification; metastability has no deterministic finite resolution bound.

Conditioned analog voltages enter the ADC. The undecided Abbott connection supplies no assumed clock, recording interface, reset or trigger source.

## Clock and state owners

| Domain | Clock | Sole responsibilities | Reset boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acquisition, A | Fixed qualified 80 MHz ±100 ppm | Tick/slot scheduler; ADC service and pin registers; BUSY recognition; DOUT capture; frame/CRC/validity; producer staging; ingress FIFO write port | Acquisition or wider destructive reset; never CPU-only reset |
| System, S | One continuously running, qualified system clock; exact frequency belongs to the build manifest | CPU-facing retained adapters; command/response and snapshot mailboxes; FIFO read port; sole block builder/writer; ring manager, leases and response sinks | System reset; CPU-only reset affects the hart, not these owners |
| Supervisor, W | Independent qualified 10 MHz ±100 ppm reference | Sticky stop/reset/isolation requests; progress/deadline watchdogs; reset/link coordination; surviving reset evidence | System reset/reconfiguration can erase this FPGA state; independent external isolation must then hold |
| CPU hart | S clock with execution enables in the first binding | Software execution and local architectural state | CPU-only reset may clear the hart; it cannot reset S's peripheral or ownership state |
| External memory/transport PHY, if present | Explicitly qualified by its implementation | Physical transactions behind S's retained tagged bridge | PHY/target reset requires transaction containment; no implicit cancellation |

A, S and W are asynchronous to each other unless the exact build proves and constrains a relationship. Frequency equality or a common oscillator does not by itself authorize a synchronous crossing. No other clock domain, external sample clock, dynamic clock mux or CPU frequency-changing mode is admitted by this supplement. Additional PHY clocks must be enumerated, crossed through an owned bridge, timed and included in service/reset qualification before the build is available.

A runs continuously in SAFE, READY, RUN, STOPPING, DRAINING and retained CLOSED/FAULT states while its clock is healthy. Use clock enables for the 1600-tick cadence, pulse widths, serial edges and timeout counters. SCLK is a registered **output waveform**, not a fabric clock: its four-tick period and two-tick half periods are generated in A; all receive logic remains clocked by A. Do not gate A, derive a clock from a counter bit, switch PLL ratios or reset the tick counter to stop recording. CPU WFI/halt changes execution permission, not acquisition or peripheral clock progress. A changed or failed clock/timing model ends the epoch and requires requalification.

Use device clock resources for distribution and the qualified PLL primitives when needed. Live/nonsticky PLL LOCK and independent progress evidence are both required; LOCK alone cannot authorize recording. The Lattice guide distinguishes live and sticky LOCK behavior and documents PLL reset/clock controls. [Lattice sysCLOCK guide, FPGA-TN-02200-1.4, §§16 and 18.3](https://www.latticesemi.com/view_document?document_id=50465).

## Crossings and identities

| Information | Crossing | Required method | Acceptance point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary command, admission reply and final completion | S ↔ A | Separate retained bundled-data mailboxes; no multibit bit-synchronizer | Whole tagged command latched once in A; admission and operation completion remain different results |
| Snapshot request and response | S ↔ A, or S ↔ W | Frozen source snapshot and retained reply mailbox | Requested source edge; CPU later reads the immutable copy |
| Complete record | A → S | Dual-clock FIFO of complete entries under one immutable epoch binding | Successful write-port ready/valid handshake transfers one entry; successful read-port handshake transfers it once |
| Producer closure watermark | A → S | Separate retained mailbox | Entire watermark latched; final receipt waits for its record prefix and downstream ownership resolution |
| Emergency STOP, CPU-reset/halt stop and inhibition receipt | S/W ↔ A | Dedicated retained stop path, independent of ordinary commands and completions | A's explicit stop/inhibit action; receipt identifies that action, not merely receipt of a wire level |
| Terminal closure receipt | S/W → requester | Retained complete terminal result | Defined closed outcome, with containment/quarantine status; never inferred from empty or reset |
| IRQ pending | Owner → CPU/S | Synchronized level; coherent cause reads and tagged source-domain clears | IRQ is notification only; it transfers no record, lease or acknowledgment |
| BUSY, live LOCK, power/qualification levels | Pins/other domains → owner | Dedicated control synchronizers; sticky capture/stretching where a pulse could disappear | Qualified registered observation, subject to the rules below |
| DOUTA | ADC → A | Timed input register with acquisition-clock capture enable | Exact scheduled falling-SCLK marker with closed setup/hold paths |
| Memory requests/responses and lease operations | S ↔ another clocked endpoint | Owned asynchronous bridge/FIFO with stable transaction identity | Explicit acceptance and matching terminal response/containment; no unowned ready/valid wires across clocks |

Every transaction-bearing recording mailbox/bridge exchange carries a nonzero `link_generation` in addition to the recording session and the operation's identity. The key is `(session_id, physical_link_id, link_generation, operation_id)`, with epoch/configuration where relevant. Scalar BUSY/LOCK/reset/permit/IRQ levels carry no such fields: their named synchronizers and initialized owner state determine interpretation, and inhibiting fault levels may act regardless of session. The coordinator allocates generations monotonically within a session; zero means DOWN, and exhaustion inhibits reuse until new-session recovery. FIFO binding replacement gets a new generation even when no hardware reset occurred. Link generation is an **internal transport incarnation**, not recording epoch, stream block sequence or memory-allocation generation; it is not added to the frozen on-wire layouts.

A mailbox admission sequence, a snapshot request sequence and a STOP receipt sequence belong to separate explicitly named channels. All are nonzero u64, nonwrapping; no channel interprets another channel's number as its own. Echo the complete key on responses. Equal low words on RV32 are not a match. Link readiness and the expected key must be established before consuming any request or reply. Stale/duplicate traffic cannot advance a counter, clear pending state, release storage or arm the ADC; retain fault/reconciliation evidence rather than relabeling it as current work.

## Retained mailbox protocol

Use a four-phase level handshake for each direction. Request and acknowledgment controls each pass through a dedicated two-stage synchronizer in their receiving domain, followed by registered protocol decisions. Do not synchronize every payload bit, OR independent synchronizer replicas, distribute the first stage, or turn an unsynchronized pulse into a clock.

1. In ONLINE idle, both sides have observed request=0 and acknowledgment=0 for the current generation. The sender reserves its response capacity, writes the complete payload, and keeps it stable. It raises request no earlier than the next source edge.
2. After seeing synchronized request=1, the receiver waits one further local edge, captures the whole payload, validates the key and applies command precedence once. It retains the complete reply and raises acknowledgment. A transport acknowledgment means the named payload was captured; it is not a successful command admission or successful operation unless the reply says so.
3. The sender observes synchronized acknowledgment=1 and captures the held reply with the same settling rule. It lowers request. Request payload remains stable through the return-to-zero exchange.
4. The receiver observes synchronized request=0 and lowers acknowledgment. The sender observes acknowledgment=0 before releasing/replacing the payload or offering another transfer. Receiver state prevents a held request from executing twice. Reply delivery storage remains owned until its separate identified consumer acknowledgment, where the contract requires one.

The bundled payload and reply paths need a routed maximum-delay proof: their latest bit arrival plus setup and uncertainty must precede the earliest permitted capture edge. The extra wait edge is not a substitute for that proof. The implementation must meet the existing ≤0.8 µs normal/≤1 µs failed-access bound including synchronization, settling, reply and local bus delivery. Return-to-zero occupies the channel until complete; a subsequent access receives bounded backpressure/access failure rather than overwriting it. A receiver may coherently reject a malformed request, but a broken transport returns **access failure/outcome unknown**, not a fabricated `INVALID_REQUEST`, `OK` or completion.

The endpoints, synchronizers, reply storage and exposed-request wrappers are outside CPU-only reset. A CPU reset cannot lower an already exposed request, discard a response sink or reset acknowledgment history. A reset CPU loses architectural delivery rights to that old request; the retained adapter still resolves it and exposes its outcome for reconciliation after boot. It does not deliver the old response as the result of a newly issued instruction. Software obtains the retained next-command sequence and outstanding results before issuing new commands; it must not restart sequences at one in the old session. Timeouts stop further admission and preserve ownership until resolution or positive containment. They never authorize automatic replay of a possibly accepted ARM or write.

The command's actual A-domain acceptance decides its outcome. A STOP/CPU-reset level observed on the acceptance edge vetoes ARM under the contract. If ARM was accepted earlier, one epoch exists and must close; later reset cannot rewrite history as rejected ARM. A CPU-reset request can take time to cross: any real trigger emitted before A observes stop belongs to that old epoch and is accounted, not silently deleted. The independent stop path stays asserted until A's retained action receipt or independent isolation establishes the outcome.

A CONFIGURE request transfers the immutable descriptor/approved-image reference and its identity, not an unbounded manifest byte stream in one access. The referenced bytes remain owned and immutable until operation completion/containment; CPU reset cannot release or overwrite them. Descriptor transfer, parsing and validation belong to the bounded configuration phases. A concrete MMIO binding must declare fixed envelope capacities and storage for all retained channels; exceeding them rejects admission. The ≤0.8 µs bound concerns command transport, not full configuration execution.

## Coherent status and interrupt handling

READ_STATUS reserves a snapshot response slot and sends a unique capture request. On one source edge, capture the entire source-domain tuple: session/link/epoch/configuration identity, state, tick, counters, pending causes, FIFO/producer watermark and qualification indicators. Hold it unchanged until S copies it and completes the handshake. The RV32-facing snapshot bank stays frozen for the whole software read and is released only by its matching snapshot identity; reading low/high words independently from live counters is forbidden.

Acquisition and system-memory snapshots are not simultaneous. Return separate source/capture identities and ticks, or perform an explicit watermark join with stated semantics. A joined closure snapshot is exact only when S has consumed A's sealed prefix and resolved all its builder/write/publication owners. Intermediate occupancy and published counts can legitimately differ. Never derive loss by subtracting unrelated snapshots. Failed/stopped source clocks produce bounded access failure or explicitly stale retained observations; a timeout cannot create a fresh timestamp or zero counters.

IRQ is a level from retained source state, synchronized into S if necessary. CPU reset/halt and mask changes do not clear its source. Acknowledgments cross as coherent commands with object identity or mask; a new cause wins over same-edge clear. Clearing an IRQ neither consumes an unread event nor frees a block. Unread block/event causes reassert, and EPOCH_CLOSED remains pending until the contract's terminal acknowledgment. Reattachment reads pending state; it does not infer a lost event from an initialized synchronizer or require an IRQ edge to discover retained work.

## Complete-record FIFO and closure

Use one 256-entry dual-clock FIFO, write port A and read port S. Each internal entry is **56 bytes: frozen record bytes 0..47 followed by a little-endian u64 slot index at 48..55**. This is internal staging, not a new archive format. The immutable FIFO binding contains session, epoch, configuration, timebase, epoch-first tick, period=1600 and link generation. Infer each tick with checked `epoch_first_tick+1600*slot`; never sample a live multibit tick across domains. Both write and read acceptance require slot index equal to their respective accepted-entry count, starting at zero; an arbitrary first slot or hidden gap is rejected. Invalid samples remain complete entries with defined validity flags.

Before ARM, both ports park empty and install only the known **prepared binding**: session/configuration/timebase/period/link generation. The retained S adapter additionally records the expected ARM request identity when that request is offered; it is not predicted during CONFIGURE. A rejected ARM creates no ACE1 and cannot enable either record port. Epoch and epoch-first tick do not exist yet. Accepted ARM atomically creates ACE1 in A with first tick A+1, exactly as the frozen contract requires. It finalizes A's binding and may write that epoch's outcomes immediately. A's retained ARM completion carries the exact ACE1 to S through the dedicated reserved metadata/reply path; S's hardware copies and validates it before enabling **any FIFO read**. Neither the first slot nor this installation waits for CPU execution or host archival. Until then S is PREPARED, not record-ready; queued entries retain A's one immutable binding. Failure triggers stop/containment and leaves S unable to accept unbound entries. It cannot silently delay the first slot or guess the epoch origin. No second ARM/context can coexist in the FIFO.

ACE1 hardware installation must complete within **24 A ticks of ARM normally, with failure action at 32 ticks**; qualify this stricter suballocation inside the existing ≤0.8 µs complete command request/reply bound. Matching installation on the inclusive deadline wins; a later installation faults. Allow at most 32 further A ticks for pointer/data visibility and transfer, including clock rounding, starting when the binding is installed, the entry is accepted and the builder is free. Thus an immediate slot-zero placeholder has a conservative **56-tick normal** envelope, leaving 8 ticks before the 64-tick normal allowance and 24 before the 80-tick timeout. Installation accepted at tick 32 gives a **64-tick worst accepted** envelope, still 16 ticks before timeout. Metadata waiting is included. Ordinary frames arriving later are measured from their actual eligibility, not required by ARM+56. A failed binding transfer cannot retroactively reject the already accepted ARM; it faults and closes that epoch with available evidence.

The end-to-end handoff interval starts at the later of the actual accepted-write edge and the builder-free edge. The first builder is reserved free before ARM. Later transfers use the same semantic eligibility instant even if its remote event is not yet locally observed. A local watchdog starting after observation must subtract its proven worst crossing/observation/clock-rounding delay from the original 80-tick allowance; it cannot restart when the pointer or metadata becomes visible. The normal 64-tick bound likewise includes that delay. The concrete CDC/timer implementation must prove these bounds rather than assuming an instantaneous cross-domain observation.

The 256 entries consume 14,336 bytes (14 KiB), leaving at most 2 KiB of the existing 16 KiB ingress allocation for binding copies, pointers, synchronizers, watermark/control storage and the declared staging registers. The implemented allocation must enumerate these bytes. No additional hidden record queue is authorized. A read output register must remain charged to a FIFO entry until its accepted transfer or to the existing sole builder afterward. The existing maximum unresolved tail stays **514 records**, with no extra prefetch owner. The block builder must finish/dispose of the old epoch before a new ARM; published old blocks and consumer leases can persist outside this FIFO binding.

Local binary pointers address the FIFO; registered Gray pointers cross through dedicated synchronizers and generate conservative local full/empty status. Never cross a binary pointer/count bit by bit. Prove full-before-write, empty-before-read, stable whole-entry data and exactly one pointer advance per successful local handshake. Registered Gray transitions and their routed max delay/skew must ensure the receiver cannot combine transitions from different source advances; apply a bound strictly below the shortest qualified source pointer-update interval, including uncertainty. A false-path declaration alone does not establish that property.

Remote pointer observation is delayed. The write-side occupancy estimate may overestimate use; this safely triggers high-water stop early. It must never invent a free credit. Conservative read empty may delay consumption within the handoff budget. CPU-visible occupancy uses a coherent snapshot rather than independently synchronized binary counts. RAM bytes need not clear on reset, and EBR/FIFO reset is not proof that payload has been erased. Only current binding, initialized pointers, current valid state and ownership authorize a read. [Lattice memory guide, FPGA-TN-02204-1.6, §§4.1.2 and 6](https://www.latticesemi.com/view_document?document_id=50466).

The A producer keeps a checked, nonwrapping accepted-entry count within the epoch. After stopping, it first resolves its one pending outcome and staged offer, then seals the final accepted FIFO count W and last accepted slot (separate known/empty indicator). Any slot it cannot enqueue is accounted as loss or uncertainty; W counts transfers, not scheduled slots. Once a slot is finally classified unavailable, the transferable prefix ends: later outcomes cannot resume enqueue after that gap and must be loss/uncertainty-accounted too. The complete binding, W and accounting status cross through the independent closure mailbox. A then makes no more writes under that binding.

S may receive the watermark before the last record becomes visible through pointer synchronization. It waits for exactly W accepted read transfers, validates their ordered slots, and resolves the builder, DMA, header/CRC visibility and publication. It rejects an impossible watermark, extra entry, duplicate identity or changed binding. Empty alone is never a drain acknowledgment. The combined final watermark crossing and record handoff remains ≤2 µs under healthy service; normal handoff is ≤64 A ticks, timeout 80. Final closure retains the existing hard 10 ms independent bound. Missing source acknowledgment invokes containment using surviving evidence; it never synthesizes W=0. Resetting pointers is not a way to drain.

FIFO context can change only after both ports are disabled, the current accepted prefix is consumed or explicitly loss-accounted under containment, all read-output/staging owners are resolved, and the previous builder binding is retired. The coordinator then performs the joint link-start protocol below. One-sided pointer reset, live FIFO clear, speculative context switch, or accepting new-generation entries while old data remains is unsupported.

## STOP receipts and independent progress

Emergency STOP has its own retained channel and response storage. A held CPU-reset/halt level or hardware fault asserts a sticky stop cause without requiring firmware, an ordinary-command slot, event space or FIFO capacity. Repeated STOP requests for one closure join its original nonwrapping stop identity and add causes; they do not start a second drain or replace the earliest stop boundary. An unrelated old receipt cannot acknowledge a new STOP.

Distinguish three observations: request transport capture; **trigger inhibition** with epoch/stop identity and known/unknown boundary; and **terminal closure** with the final accounting/ownership outcome. Inhibition does not promise that an emitted pulse, serial frame, FIFO tail or DMA has finished. The contract's STOP completion is the terminal outcome, not the earlier receipt. Store each observation coherently. Source/requester reset cannot turn a return-to-zero edge into any of these acknowledgments. Before another ARM, the prior terminal must be archived/acknowledged, the old STOP channel must return to mutually observed idle, and the stop/reset levels must be clear. Clearing a receipt alone does not clear the stop cause.

An active stop request is distinct from sticky historical cause evidence. When its physical request/reset/halt levels are clear and its matching final reply is copied/acknowledged, the retained bridge may finish return-to-zero and retire that request. An epoch closure additionally requires its terminal archival/acknowledgment before new ARM. A no-epoch STOP in SAFE/READY/CLOSED returns the contract's `ALREADY_STOPPED` and requires no invented epoch or terminal record; any real prior terminal remains governed by its own acknowledgment. CONFIGURING cancellation similarly retains the cancelled operation's completion. Historical stop causes remain readable after active-request retirement but are not an eternal ARM veto. Live physical faults still inhibit service/recording, and latched qualification faults require the existing RECOVER procedure after containment; retiring a STOP request does not clear them.

While clocks are healthy, an ordinary stop finishes the already emitted legal CONVST pulse and resolves its frame by the original trigger+1120 deadline. If stop and a due slot coincide, retain the defined untriggered placeholder/loss outcome; no later slots exist. A physical fault may truncate the pulse or prevent its observation: preserve that uncertainty and isolate, never label it a normal untriggered or valid conversion without evidence. Completing an old frame does not emit a new trigger.

W monitors A and S concurrently using **one outstanding challenge/response and one independent watchdog per target**, with a held sequence/tag and bounded response through retained control synchronizers. Only a target's own matching fresh response refreshes its watchdog; S activity cannot conceal A failure or conversely. Do not sample an 80 MHz toggling bit at 10 MHz: aliasing can look like no progress. Probe responders live in the target's clock/reset-control island, outside its functional acquisition/CPU reset, so clock validation can proceed while data-plane resets and fences remain held. Probe identity is `(supervisor_lifetime, target, probe_sequence)`, not a recording-session command. Sequence is nonzero u64 and never wraps; exhaustion removes recording permit before reuse. Supervisor lifetime is replaced only with coordinated initialization of both probe endpoints under external isolation and a fresh idle baseline before any response counts; a functional reset cannot clear probe history.

A response must be generated by the target domain after observing that challenge. W consumes each response once and offers the next challenge within one W tick after the channel returns to idle. Normal fresh-response spacing must be ≤20 W ticks. The **80-W-tick** watchdog runs through every phase, including return-to-zero, from startup or the last matching fresh response; partial, duplicate and stale activity never restarts it. After target-clock loss, at most one already-generated response can arrive and refresh it. Qualify that last arrival within 1 µs of loss and final detection/isolation propagation within 0.5 µs. Thus the existing bound remains `80*T_Wmax+1.5 µs=9.500800 µs`, leaving 0.499200 µs before 10 µs. These are implementation admission limits, not measured delays. An unqualified probe schedule inhibits recording; PLL LOCK is not its replacement.

The existing separate external analog recording-permit watchdog covers a stopped W clock. Its passive isolation behavior, rail sequencing and restoration are hardware qualification gates. No stopped target clock is used to time its own recovery, and no software ISR is on this path.

## Reset assertion, link start and output release

Reset-request/isolation assertion must work without the failed target clock. Safety-critical permit removal and ingress/egress fences assert independently. **Crossing-visible pointers, handshake history and outstanding-owner state must not clear before both endpoints are parked or independently confirmed fenced.** A reset request can immediately kill new local enables while retaining that crossing state. During asynchronous parking, an unfenced peer may still transfer under the old binding; those accepted transfers belong in its final parked watermark/transaction journal.

After the fence barrier, local destructive control resets assert asynchronously and deassert through one qualified reset synchronizer per domain. For abrupt loss that prevents staged assertion, the physical reset/fence distribution must demonstrably disable peer acceptance before any reset-induced pointer/handshake change can be consumed. Include synchronizer delay, reset skew and the destination acceptance register in that proof. Ambiguous boundary work is quarantined/uncertain, never accepted as a new valid record or acknowledgment. A build lacking this ordering guarantee is unsupported; merely connecting the same reset net to two clock domains does not prove it.

Hold reset until minimum primitive pulse requirements are met and local clock health is established. Deassert only after at least two live local edges through the release synchronizer, with recovery/removal and reset-fanout timing verified. Shared-clock reconvergent logic uses one coordinated local reset tree; independently released replicas must not disagree about ownership. RAM payload reset is unnecessary because all validity is revoked.

Reset release is not ONLINE, READY or RUN. The coordinator performs the following disarmed barrier for every affected link:

1. Block new offers/acceptances at **both** ends, inhibit triggers, and latch the reset reason before destructive state clearing when possible. An accepted or already exposed external transaction retains its response sink and owner outside the reset scope, or the target is positively isolated. A reset request never withdraws a visible transaction on its own.
2. Obtain tagged park acknowledgments from live endpoints, including read-output/staging state. For a dead endpoint, use independently confirmed isolation instead; its unseen contents become lost/uncertain, not an exact empty claim. Quarantine any effects not positively contained. No uncontained transaction can reach a newly reused allocation.
3. With both ends isolated, clear local pointer/handshake validity and synchronizer histories together. On each restarted clock, keep data-plane enables off until local release, control pipelines settling and observed request/ack idle baseline are complete. Clearing only one side is forbidden even if the other side's clock is stopped.
4. Install the fresh nonzero generation and known immutable context through a supervisor-controlled coherent bootstrap exchange. Each endpoint returns the installed key; mismatched/stale acknowledgments leave the link DOWN. For the record FIFO this is the prepared binding, not a pre-invented ACE1. The bootstrap is allowed only under isolation and cannot execute an ADC command or grant a lease.
5. After both installation acknowledgments and idle baselines are confirmed, authorize each endpoint ONLINE for exactly that key. One side may see ONLINE earlier; its peer still refuses transfers until locally ONLINE. Record ports additionally require the ARM-created binding as specified above: A must have accepted ARM before writing, and S must have received ACE1 before reading. Do not replay any pre-reset payload. Explicit CONFIGURE/RECOVER and later ARM remain necessary; ONLINE alone has no recording side effect.

The coordinator, bootstrap response sinks and isolation evidence must survive the scoped reset they coordinate. If that authority is itself lost, escalate to system-reset/new-session recovery with external isolation; do not reconstruct it from reset values. All active park/rejoin/setup waits are disarmed and have a ≤500 ms wall-time failure bound within the existing configuration startup allocation; configuration as a whole still has its independent three-second bound. A held external reset or unconfirmed quarantine can remain indefinitely only with the recorder disarmed, externally isolated and passively supervised. Clock disappearance during a barrier leaves DOWN/FAULT, not a partly initialized active link.

Before removing ADC isolation, initialize and observe internal `CONVST=0`, `CS=1`, `SCLK=1`, `SDI=0` under a valid owner, with no pending pulse/serial enable. Confirm the actual safe interface/rails and stable controls, then release isolation synchronously while CONVST remains low. Raising a permit while a hidden CONVST register is high would create a phantom trigger and is forbidden. Idle release itself cannot request service or ARM. Physical isolation may alter CS/SCLK during a fault; all affected ADC protocol state is then unqualified and must be fully reinitialized. During FPGA configuration or loss of power, safe pins require qualified external circuitry, not assumptions about FPGA reset defaults.

BUSY's synchronizer and edge history start invalid, not as an observed high/fall. Before ARM or a service conversion, establish a qualified synchronized low baseline with no outstanding frame; require at least four further A ticks of low after the synchronizer pipeline is initialized. Match a post-trigger observed high followed by low to that one owned slot; an initial low, reset-cleared high or stale transition cannot complete it. Preserve the four-tick observation allocation and trigger+20/+160 deadlines. A missing/late/illegal transition closes the epoch; it cannot be reassigned to the next slot.

DOUT does not traverse that synchronizer. The A-clock input capture enable occurs on the exact falling-SCLK marker, with the first-bit CS setup, repeated half-cycle setup, hold, last-bit and CS-disable paths closed under the existing I/O budget. A healthy ADC CRC cannot substitute for that proof or restore validity after a clock/reset fault.

## Reset scopes and record consequences

| Reset | Retained authority/state | Invalidated or unresolved data | Outstanding work and required restart |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CPU-only reset** | A tick/session/epoch/configuration, counters, staged/FIFO records; S mailboxes/completions/snapshots, builder/DMA/response sinks, ring generations and leases; W stop/fault state | Does not invalidate already complete records merely because the CPU restarted. The active epoch stops; due/in-flight slots follow ordinary stop rules. CONFIGURING is cancelled and its qualification invalidated. CPU-private unsaved software state and architectural response delivery are lost. | Retained wrappers finish or contain old operations. Reboot reconciles coherent outstanding state before new commands. If reset interrupted RUN, STOPPING or DRAINING, finish closure/archive and full RECOVER/CONFIGURE before explicit ARM. In unchanged SAFE/READY/CLOSED, preserve permitted qualification/evidence but recheck health and attach; CLOSED still requires recovery. No automatic restart; held reset vetoes ARM. |
| **Acquisition-logic reset** | Healthy S/W retain published blocks/leases, transaction journals, already copied complete records and available old-session observations; external archive survives | A tick/slot/capture/ADC service state and qualification are destroyed. Any incomplete/staged/untransferred A result is unusable. Queued FIFO data affected by reset is lost unless ownership and complete pre-reset transfer to retained S are proven. Old unpublished work cannot be reinterpreted under a fresh binding. | Fence both FIFO ports and affected mailboxes. Reconcile old writes/publication and quarantine uncertainty; seal old outcome when evidence permits, otherwise archive INCOMPLETE. Destroyed counters/timing require a **new unique session, new timebase and fresh link generations**, complete ADC startup/analog/clock/CDC qualification, then explicit ARM. Old leases stay old; they are never freed by resetting A. |
| **System reset** | Only independently retained external isolation/target containment and durably archived evidence are assumed; no FPGA volatile journal or counter is trusted to survive | All A/S/W validity, handshake history and volatile recording ownership are invalid. Raw RAM/flash bytes that physically survive are not a new valid FIFO, descriptor lease or terminal record. Unarchived tail is INCOMPLETE unless an independently retained authoritative terminal proves its outcome. | Isolate ADC and bus masters; positively drain/fence/reset targets before reusing address space. Reconcile externally trusted evidence, initialize memory validity/ownership and all links, establish new session/timebase, reload firmware and perform full qualification. If target effects remain unknown, remain isolated/FAULT. |
| **FPGA reconfiguration** | Durable external archive and explicitly qualified external reset/isolation circuitry only | Assume no internal retention and no continuously driven FPGA pin value. All logic, memory/CDC validity and bitstream-specific timing/qualification are lost. An interrupted recording has the same incomplete-tail rule as system reset. | Require system-reset containment before configuration when planned; unexpected reconfiguration invokes external fail-safe isolation. Validate the loaded image, primitive/pin map and reset behavior, reestablish target containment and new session/timebase/links, then full qualification and explicit ARM. No old PUB1 or RAM content grants ownership. |

These scopes are behavioral contracts, not interchangeable names for one reset wire. A reset of memory-controller/fabric ownership state has system-reset severity unless a retained wrapper demonstrably preserves every offered/accepted transaction and lease. A lost clock, PLL relock or unknown reset that destroys A timing uses acquisition-reset severity at minimum. Multiple resets take the union of invalidated state and the strongest containment requirement; a CPU-only label cannot hide a shared-clock/rail or wider reset effect.

An **ADC RESET pin pulse is separate from acquisition-logic reset**. It is a controlled, full reset only during stopped CONFIGURE/RECOVER, with the ADC profile's pulse/wait/register sequence; it does not clear engine session, counters, ownership or transport history. Unexpected ADC reset/status invalidates affected conversion evidence, terminates the epoch and requires recovery. Partial ADC reset is not an R1 recording/recovery shortcut. Service test conversions remain tagged service work and never enter the recording FIFO.

For any reset, a complete pre-boundary record already transferred into a surviving owner keeps its captured meaning if its timing/analog validity was established. A reset alone does not retroactively corrupt immutable published bytes. If the reset/clock/analog onset is uncertain, retain quality/uncertainty amendments for the potentially affected interval; do not silently rewrite VALID in published blocks. An unpublished partial frame is either represented by its canonical invalid outcome while a trustworthy owner remains, or included in exact loss/uncertainty accounting. Never synthesize a valid zero sample to repair a reset gap.

Publication can straddle reset. If PUB1 was offered but completion is unknown, the retained S owner must establish exactly-once old-session visibility/commit or positively contain the target. Until then, quarantine the allocation and keep its publication/accounting outcome uncertain. A timeout or CRC-valid readback alone does not prove the original ownership transition; proof must include the matching retained transaction, completed payload/header, visibility and absence of late writes. If all authority was erased, recover only through independently trusted external evidence/containment; old memory cannot certify its own lease.

No timeout or reset frees a lease. CPU-owned reads must finish or be positively contained before their leases can be retired. A late response may resolve its original quarantined transaction once, but cannot satisfy a new transaction, restart a frame timer, deliver a record twice or release a reused generation. Completion sinks and old identity records stay until that obligation is resolved. A new session changes namespaces; it does not cancel outstanding old electrical transactions.

Planned destructive reset first requests ordinary STOP, archives the terminal and amendments, retires/contains all old traffic and asserts isolation. Abrupt reset may erase the terminal before this is possible. In that case the recorder explicitly archives surviving evidence as **INCOMPLETE**, records the absent terminal/unknown tail and authorizes new-session recovery only after containment. Do not fabricate ACK_TERMINAL for an erased object or wait forever for an acknowledgment that cannot exist. This exception permits recovery, not a clean-closure claim. Retained published old-session blocks can be archived under their original trustworthy binding; their generations cannot be reassigned until old accesses are retired.

## Review and release gates

| Required property | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| No phantom trigger | Reset/stop at every cadence/pulse edge; no ARM on reset release or link ONLINE; safe-low permit release; only owned service or scheduled recording pulses |
| No phantom or duplicated record | FIFO wrap/full/empty, delayed pointers, every reset phase, retained read-output state, stale generation and context replacement; transferred record identities are a subset of real offered complete outcomes |
| No phantom acknowledgment | All four mailbox phases and independent clock orderings; CPU reset before/after acceptance; old/duplicate replies, interrupted bootstrap and stale STOP receipt; explicit operation/action identity |
| No ownership change from reset alone | Reset/timeout during exposed request, accepted write, delayed response, PUB1 commit and held lease; quarantine until matching completion/positive containment |
| No unbounded active recovery | Control/handoff/frame/drain/configuration watchdogs plus stopped A/S/W fault injection; passive quarantine remains inhibited |
| Physical CDC/RDC feasibility | Exact domain/reset inventory; synchronizer/Gray/bundled-data netlist review; routed delay/skew, reset recovery/removal and clock-distribution reports; calculated MTBF with stated target; measured pin/permit behavior |

The [offline checker](check_lifecycle.py) explores finite abstract handshake, FIFO, STOP and transaction-ownership models under independent edge/reset choices and retains counterexamples for deliberately unsafe controls. It is not RTL, a metastability simulator, an unbounded proof of the whole engine or evidence of a physical reset circuit. Its [review evidence](evidence/lifecycle-2026-11-05/review.json) states the explored state spaces, assumptions, revisions and actual outcomes. The existing single-clock synthetic guard and P25 CPU image do not implement these crossings.

The open ECP5 flow must explicitly verify preservation/placement of synchronizers, full reset scopes and every applicable max/min path; an `async_reg` attribute or internal Fmax result alone is insufficient. nextpnr documents a limited ECP5 LPF interface and clock constraints; unsupported external/CDC timing constraints cannot be treated as enforced. Use reviewed routed-path extraction/composed constraints where required, consistent with the timing budget. [nextpnr constraint documentation](https://github.com/YosysHQ/nextpnr/blob/main/docs/constraints.md).

Until the implemented design and exact board satisfy those gates, the result is a defined lifecycle with abstract protocol evidence, and `ACQ-R1_AVAILABLE` remains false.
